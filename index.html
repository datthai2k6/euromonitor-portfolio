Data Analyst (Course Project) · Jun 2026 – Jul 2026

Spartan Race Social Media Performance Audit

Selected for ACE Academy Showcase Data StorytellingSocial Media MarketingPerformance ReportingExcelData Cleaning

Spartan Race published 393 Facebook posts, but format mix, posting times and content pillars did not match how its audience behaved. Two dashboard metrics were also wrong. I audited the page and built a 4-pillar plan: shift format, realign schedule, scale athlete stories, clean the data.

What I found

  • Format. Video was 49.62% of all posts but averaged 734 interactions per post. Photos averaged 945, 22.3% higher. Video still led on share rate (9.6%).
  • Timing. 54.71% of posts went out in the evening (724 avg.). Morning carried 4.07% of volume and averaged 909. Saturday averaged 1,248.89 per post; Thursday and Friday carried the most volume for less return.
  • Pillars. Athlete Stories & Coaching was 20 of 393 posts (5%) yet earned a 10.99% share rate, above Race Experience at 6.92%.
  • Data integrity. "Status" showed a 37.89% comment rate from only 2 posts. "Promotions & Partnerships" was inflated by one duplicated post logged at about 10x its live numbers.

Post format

Publishing timing

What I recommended

Cut routine video volume by 30% and move it to high-impact photos. Keep video only for share and comment goals, with a 3-second hook and a clear call to action. Move key posts to Tuesdays, Saturdays and the 12h–13h lunch slot. Triple Athlete Stories from 20 to 60–80 posts per cycle. Purge the duplicate record, add anomaly alerts for any post deviating more than 15% from live platform data, and freeze promotional budget until the dataset is validated.

The finding that mattered most was not a winning format. It was that two of the dashboard's "best performers" were data errors.

Capstone Project – Consumer Behavior: Cielo Stellato Mini

Invited by the brand's executive team Digital MarketingMarket & Competitor ResearchTVC ProductionPitchingConsumer InsightsStrategic Campaign Planning

Cielo Stellato entered Vietnam's feminine hygiene market with under 1% share against Diana (41%) and Kotex (38%). Its product claims were strong, but most women could not recall the brand. Our team turned consumer research into an emotional TVC concept.

Research

  • Market. $508.0M in 2024, projected $895.1M by 2030 (StrategyHelix, 2025).
  • Awareness. 70% of 64 respondents could not recognise or recall Cielo. Those aware had mostly only heard the name. Social media ads drove 65% of awareness.
  • Category behaviour. Purchase is habit-driven and loyal. Emotional benefits and social proof outweigh functional claims.

Insight to idea

  • Insight. "I am a strong woman, but I need space to be vulnerable. I want a brand that respects my feelings, not one that tells me to just be strong and move on."
  • Big idea. Gửi gắm sự dịu dàng, vỗ về nỗi lo âu, built on one contrast: visible pain gets attention, silent pain does not.
  • Key message. Đồng hành cùng bạn, xoa dịu những ngày không dễ dàng.

Capstone Project – Business Analytics: Mother & Baby Retailer

Featured at UEH.ISB Project Showcase Team LeadershipAdvanced ExcelPower BIData Visualization & Reporting

Four years of data from a mother & baby retail chain: 13K+ orders and 1,800+ products across stores and an online shop. I led a team of 6, split the work into nine questions across sales, customers and operations, cleaned the dataset in Power Query and ran the customer analysis.

What my team found

  • Channel imbalance & returns. In-store drives 93% of sales ($76.53M). Online generates just 7% ($6.18M) but causes 62% of return volume.
  • Revenue concentration. 8 Class A product types drive 89% ($73.6M) of total revenue, while 84 Class B & C SKUs combined contribute only 11%.
  • Basket & rush hour patterns. Orders average 1–3 items ($6.24K AOV). In-store rush hours hit 11 AM–5 PM, while Sunday sales crash near zero.

Channel and product mix

Switch the metric. Hover or tap a slice for detail.

    What my team recommended

    • Fix online returns. Standardise product listings and packaging to cut online's 62% share of returns; run online deals to shift peak store traffic.
    • Rationalise catalogue. Focus marketing budget on the 8 Class A bestsellers and phase out low-margin Class C items (1.04% of sales).
    • Bundle & optimise shifts. Pair slow movers with Baby Formula to target $3K–$7.3K order values; reallocate staff to 11 AM–5 PM rush hours and activate Sunday promotions.
    Transformed 4 years of retail data into strategy: reallocated peak-hour store staffing, targeted 84 underperforming SKUs for phase-out, and fixed product listing inaccuracies behind online's 62% share of returns.
